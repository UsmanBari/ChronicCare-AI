## Source Verification

### Source 1 — ada_2026_soc_sec6

**Status:** VERIFIED

**Original source:** American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes. "6. Glycemic Goals, Hypoglycemia, and Hyperglycemic Crises: Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026." Diabetes Care. 2026;49(Suppl. 1):S132–S149.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-S006

**Verification:** The original publication supports the interview-bank claims. It defines Level 1 hypoglycemia as glucose below 70 mg/dL but at least 54 mg/dL and Level 2 hypoglycemia as glucose below 54 mg/dL. It also includes CGM metrics such as time in range, time above range, and time below range, and recommends reviewing hypoglycemia history in at-risk individuals.

**Relevant sections:** "Glycemic Assessment" and "Hypoglycemia Assessment, Prevention, and Treatment."

### Source 2 — ada_2026_soc_sec5

**Status:** VERIFIED

**Original source:** American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes. "5. Facilitating Positive Health Behaviors and Well-being to Improve Health Outcomes: Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026." Diabetes Care. 2026;49(Suppl. 1):S89–S131.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-S005

**Verification:** The original publication supports the interview-bank claims. It recommends routine psychosocial screening, including screening for diabetes distress, and discusses barriers that can affect diabetes treatment and self-management. It also recommends screening for diabetes distress at least annually and repeating screening when treatment goals are not met, during transitions, or when diabetes complications are present.

**Relevant sections:** "Psychosocial Care," "Screening," and "Diabetes Distress."

### Source 3 — ada_2026_soc_sec12

**Status:** VERIFIED

**Original source:** American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes. "12. Retinopathy, Neuropathy, and Foot Care: Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026." Diabetes Care. 2026;49(Suppl. 1):S261–S276.

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-S012

**Verification:** The original publication supports the interview-bank claims. It recommends assessment for diabetic peripheral neuropathy and annual 10-g monofilament testing to identify feet at risk for ulceration and amputation. It also recommends a comprehensive foot evaluation at least annually to identify risk factors for ulcers and amputations. The foot examination includes skin inspection, assessment of foot deformities, neurological assessment, and vascular assessment. The guidance also emphasizes early recognition and surveillance of at-risk feet and foot problems.

**Relevant sections:** "Neuropathy — Screening" and "Foot Care."

### Source 4 — aha_acc_2025_hypertension

**Status:** VERIFIED

**Original source:** 2025 AHA/ACC Clinical Practice Guideline for the Prevention, Detection, Evaluation, and Management of High Blood Pressure in Adults.

**Verification:** The guideline supports the interview-bank claims concerning standardized blood pressure measurement and home blood pressure monitoring, evaluation of medications that can interfere with blood pressure control, and severe hypertension thresholds. It identifies severe hypertension at blood pressure above 180/120 mmHg and distinguishes hypertensive emergency by the presence of acute target-organ damage. The guideline also recommends careful medication review when evaluating resistant hypertension.

**Relevant sections:** Blood pressure measurement/home monitoring, secondary forms of hypertension and resistant hypertension, and Section 6.2 "Hypertensive Emergencies and Severe Hypertension in Nonpregnant and Nonstroke Patients."

### Source 5 — idf_dar_2021_ramadan

**Status:** VERIFIED

**Original source:** International Diabetes Federation and the DAR International Alliance. *Diabetes and Ramadan: Practical Guidelines*. Brussels, Belgium: International Diabetes Federation; 2021.

**Verification:** The guideline supports the interview-bank claims concerning Ramadan fasting risk stratification, pre-dawn meal (Suhoor) guidance, recognition of hypoglycaemia during fasting, and criteria for breaking the fast. It recommends individual assessment and stratification of fasting risk and provides guidance on when a person with diabetes should stop the fast because of abnormal blood glucose levels, symptoms of hypoglycaemia or hyperglycaemia, dehydration, or acute illness.

**Relevant sections:** Risk Stratification; Pre-Ramadan Assessment and Education; dietary guidance for Suhoor and Iftar; and "When to break the fast."

### Source 6 — nice_ng28_t2d

**Status:** VERIFIED

**Original source:** National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE). *Type 2 diabetes in adults: management (NG28).*

**Verification:** The current NICE NG28 guideline supports the use of clear, individualised sick-day rules for people with type 2 diabetes. These rules may include medication changes during illness, temporarily stopping relevant medicines when there is a risk of dehydration, vomiting or diarrhoea, adjustment of insulin doses, and guidance on restarting medication after recovery. NG28 also directs clinicians to MHRA safety advice on ketone monitoring during interruption of SGLT-2 inhibitor treatment.

**Relevant section:** Recommendation 1.10.1 — Sick day rules.

**Review note:** The interview-bank description also mentions fluids and ketone-monitoring alert triggers. The current NG28 text reviewed here does not directly establish all of that broader wording, so those details should not be attributed to NG28 without an additional supporting source.

### Source 7 — nice_ng136_hypertension

**Status:** VERIFIED

**Original source:** National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE). *Hypertension in adults: diagnosis and management (NG136).*

**Verification:** NICE NG136 supports the interview-bank claims concerning postural hypotension, assessment for target-organ damage, and symptom-based red flags. The guideline recommends checking for postural hypotension in relevant people with hypertension. For severe hypertension, it recommends investigation for target-organ damage and identifies symptoms or signs that require same-day specialist assessment, including retinal haemorrhage or papilloedema, new-onset confusion, chest pain, signs of heart failure, and acute kidney injury.

**Relevant sections:** Recommendations 1.1.5–1.1.7 on postural hypotension; recommendation 1.3.3 on target-organ damage; recommendations 1.5.1–1.5.3 on same-day specialist review; and recommendation 1.4.16 on postural hypotension during treatment monitoring.
